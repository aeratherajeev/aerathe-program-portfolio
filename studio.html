What moved the numbers

No single fix produced these results. They came from interconnected interventions across six areas, working together.

  1. 01

    Demand and intake

    One intake workflow and faster resource allocation. Earlier visibility into incoming demand enabled proactive staffing, more accurate sales estimates, and faster project mobilisation.

  2. 02

    Scope and commercial control

    Structured change control to bill out-of-scope work. Stronger roadmap governance and project planning accountability across PMs.

  3. 03

    Risk and escalation

    Earlier risk visibility through structured governance. Targeted recovery of 20+ escalated programs through re-baselining and stakeholder realignment.

  4. 04

    Delivery flow

    Cross-functional workflows to reduce handoff friction. Technical feasibility checkpoints to identify constraints early and reduce design rework.

  5. 05

    AI-enabled workflows

    Automated estimation, research synthesis, and repetitive production tasks. Interactive prototypes and reports replaced static deliverables, accelerating presales and freeing designers to focus on higher-value work.

  6. 06

    People and consistency

    Coaching and supporting 11 PMs. Standardised design workflows across centres, including Figma file organisation, shared knowledge, and repeatable practices.

03

Context

By 2024, Lollypop had grown into a large, multi-centre design organisation.

  • 250+designers
  • 7centres
  • 35+concurrent programs
  • 11project managers

But the operating model hadn’t kept pace with the scale.

Inside the studioExecution was fragmented

  • Each department had its own processes.
  • Work entered without clearly defined scope.
  • Resource constraints surfaced late.
  • Handoffs between design and engineering were fragmented.
  • Project risks often escalated close to deadlines.

Outside the studioThe economics of design were changing

  • Clients expected faster turnarounds.
  • Presales timelines were becoming a competitive disadvantage.
  • Designers spent considerable time on repetitive work surrounding design, rather than design itself.

The challenge was twofold: make delivery predictable at scale while fundamentally rethinking how design work gets done.

04

What I did

I approached this as an operating model redesign across three interconnected layers.

1Portfolio layer

Establishing governance and decision alignment

Fragmented conversations across Sales, Delivery, Finance, and Leadership were replaced with a structured decision-making rhythm. I organised existing meetings into purposeful governance forums.

2Delivery layer

Standardising execution across the studio

I built shared workflows connecting demand, staffing, and execution across all seven centres.

3AI transformation layer

Reengineering design workflows with Design Leadership

In partnership with Design Leadership, I helped shape and operationalise how design work moved across discovery, research, production, and presales. Together, we mapped where designer time was being spent and identified opportunities to eliminate repetitive, non-design work through AI.

The principle was simple: AI handles the mechanical work; designers focus on judgment, creativity, and problem-solving.

  • Discovery and research

    From

    Manually transcribing interviews, coding notes, and compiling findings into slide decks.

    To

    AI handles transcription, synthesis, and data processing, and findings are shared as interactive HTML reports. Researchers focus on interpreting patterns and validating insights.

  • Design production

    From

    Designers manually producing wireframes, UI variations, component states, and prototypes through iterative, time-intensive workflows.

    To

    AI accelerates ideation and production, generating interface explorations, variants, states, and realistic content. Designers spend less time on repetitive execution and more on exploring directions, refining craft, and evaluating solutions. Interactive prototypes make it possible to test ideas and validate decisions earlier.

  • Presales

    From

    Estimates built from scratch and static presentations. Proofs of concept took 2–3 weeks to develop.

    To

    AI-assisted estimation gives design leads a structured baseline to refine, while rapid prototyping compresses POC development. Instead of relying on static decks to communicate an idea, prospects can interact with a working proof of concept, making the proposed solution more tangible during early-stage discussions.

    POC turnaround: 2–3 weeks → under 5 days

A point of view

The review loop is changing

Something I think designers are underestimating: AI is becoming an invisible stakeholder in client-side decision-making. Work goes out, and the response comes back unusually structured. Clear critique, sharp callouts, sometimes alternative suggestions.

You’re no longer designing only for the client. Your work also has to pass an AI reading of quality on the decision-maker’s side, one that’s fast, pattern-trained, and unforgiving on basics.

The shiftRunning your own work through an AI critique pass before sharing it is becoming table stakes. Not as a replacement for thinking, but as baseline hygiene, because the approver of your work now has a faster way to question it.

A lot of what AI flags isn’t wrong. If an AI can flag hierarchy, logic, and usability gaps in 30 seconds, a client eventually will, and once that feedback comes externally, you’ve already lost some control of the narrative. Designers who adapt walk into reviews knowing where their work will be challenged. The ones who don’t keep defending, in real time, things they could have caught themselves in five minutes.

This isn’t about designing for AI approval. It’s about not being blindsided by AI-assisted critique.

+Enablement, supporting the AI layer

Building the infrastructure for adoption

Introducing AI workflows across a multi-centre organisation required more than tooling. It required a repeatable system for experimentation, learning, and adoption.

  1. 1

    Pilot

    Formed a cross-studio pilot team to validate workflows on live projects.

  2. 2

    Codify

    Documented successful practices into reusable playbooks.

  3. 3

    Show

    Ran cross-centre showcases demonstrating improvements in speed and output quality.

  4. 4

    Invest

    Built the business case for shared AI tooling and secured leadership investment.

  5. 5

    Sustain

    Established an enablement model that the pilot team could sustain independently.
