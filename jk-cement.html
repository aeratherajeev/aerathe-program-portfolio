02

The challenge

JK Cement was transforming seven interconnected applications spanning its partner ecosystems. The program brought together multiple organisations, vendors, and workstreams, each with different requirements, assumptions, and priorities.

What made a unified direction hard

  • Fragmented PRDs
  • Evolving business expectations
  • Unresolved technical dependencies
  • No shared success metrics

My mandate: bring clarity, alignment, and delivery predictability to a complex enterprise transformation while keeping business outcomes at the centre.

03

Four decisions that shaped the program

  1. 01

    Establishing one definition of success

    The tension

    With 240+ requirements distributed across seven applications, individual workstreams risked optimising for their own deliverables.

    What I did

    I synthesised the requirements into a shared Program Objective Map, aligning stakeholders around three measurable, ecosystem-wide KPIs.

    240+ requirements across 7 apps

    • Time to first transactionHow quickly partners reach their first successful transaction.
    • Transaction conversion rateHow effectively users progress from intent to transaction.
    • Support call volumeHow independently partners can navigate the ecosystem.

    These metrics gave Business, Design, and Engineering a common framework to evaluate requirements, prioritise the MVP, and guide decisions throughout discovery, design, and delivery.

    Outcome

    Shifted the program from delivering seven individual applications to improving partner activation, transaction success, and usability across the ecosystem.

  2. 02

    Shaping and securing the MVP

    The tension

    Competing business requirements, technical dependencies, and evolving priorities made scope difficult to stabilise.

    What I did

    I facilitated cross-functional prioritisation with business, BCG, Design, and Engineering stakeholders to align on critical launch capabilities and defer lower-priority requirements to subsequent phases.

    Outcome

    A focused MVP, aligned stakeholders, and a phased roadmap that protected time-to-value.

  3. 03

    Resolving the build-versus-platform conflict

    The tension

    Design envisioned custom experiences for CTS and SFA, while Engineering anticipated Salesforce Lightning constraints.

    What I did

    I initiated a two-week feasibility spike, using representative workflows to evaluate technical constraints, customisation, effort, and delivery implications. I then orchestrated a decision workshop across business, IT, BCG, Design, and Engineering.

    Outcome

    A hybrid architecture was agreed upon before execution, helping prevent downstream rework and potential schedule delays.

    Internal applicationsSalesforce Lightning
    Partner-facing applicationsCustom experiences
  4. 04

    Expanding scope to protect business outcomes

    The tension

    During stakeholder conversations, I identified usability issues in the legacy dealer loyalty app that could undermine engagement and retention.

    What I did

    I initiated a usability evaluation and prototyped an improved redemption experience to validate the opportunity.

    Outcome

    Secured additional client investment and integrated the loyalty app into a subsequent delivery wave without disrupting the original roadmap.

04

How the program was governed

A phase-gate model built around early risk resolution.

The program operated through defined checkpoints that aligned business requirements, technical feasibility, design maturity, and delivery readiness across workstreams.

05

How I orchestrated delivery

  1. 01

    Unified governance across five organisations

    Established shared cadences, decision forums, escalation paths, change control, sign-offs, and executive reporting.

  2. 02

    Surfaced technical dependencies early

    Coordinated API, SAP, and Salesforce data dependencies before design freeze, reducing uncertainty across teams.

  3. 03

    Protected delivery predictability

    Aligned requirements, dependencies, and phased scope across parallel workstreams against a shared roadmap.

  4. 04

    Extended UX quality into UAT

    Partnered with Design and QA to incorporate critical user journeys, interaction behaviour, exception states, and experience-related acceptance criteria into validation.
